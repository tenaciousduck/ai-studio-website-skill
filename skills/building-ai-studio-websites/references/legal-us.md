# United States notes

Not legal advice. Last checked 2026-11-02. Federal points come from FTC and FCC pages unless marked. State law varies and was mostly not read. Dollar penalties change yearly. Confirm anything that decides a design.

## Email

CAN-SPAM covers commercial email, with no exception for business-to-business mail. Unlike Canada, it doesn't require consent before sending, though the skill's consent standard is safe to keep. Each marketing email needs:

- Honest headers and subject lines, and a sender that identifies the business.
- A valid postal address, which can be a street address, a registered PO box or a registered private mailbox.
- A clear opt-out that works for at least 30 days after sending, honored within 10 business days.
- A clear statement that it's an advertisement.

Transactional messages such as order confirmations are mostly exempt, read narrowly. Each email in violation can cost up to $53,088.

## Text messages

GHL makes texting easy, so ask before adding it.

- Autodialed marketing texts need prior written consent, and that applies to small businesses.
- The FCC's "one-to-one consent" rule never took effect. A court vacated it on January 24, 2025, and law firms report the FCC later repealed it.
- Revocations must be honored within 10 business days. One confirmation text with no marketing is allowed.
- FCC 26-67, adopted September 30, 2026, lets a sender name an exclusive opt-out method from a short list, if it's clearly disclosed. Without one, any reasonable method counts. It starts 30 days after Federal Register publication, and that date wasn't confirmed here.
- The same document proposes a shorter deadline and required two-way texting, so check before building.
- From memory, unconfirmed: US carriers also require business texting registration (A2P 10DLC).

## Shipping dates

The FTC's Mail, Internet or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule applies to goods, not services.

- Any ship date you state needs a reasonable basis. With no date stated, expect to ship within 30 days.
- If you can't ship on time, offer the buyer a choice of accepting the delay or cancelling for a prompt refund, with a new date or a reason there isn't one.
- Refunds on cancelled orders are due within seven working days, in full, including shipping. Store credit can't replace them.
- Keep records. Penalties can reach $53,088 per violation.

The FTC's page doesn't say how this applies to a seller outside the US. A "no cancellations" line can't override it for goods that haven't shipped, so a US-facing seller should plan for late-order refunds.

## Subscriptions

ROSCA is a statute, and it requires clear disclosure, express consent and a simple way to cancel for anything that renews. The FTC's broader 2024 "click to cancel" rule was vacated in court on July 8, 2025, and the FTC asked for new comments in March 2026. Check status before building a subscription. Gym and similar membership contracts may have their own state rules, which weren't read, so check the client's state.

## Reviews and testimonials

The FTC's rule (16 CFR Part 465), in force since October 21, 2024, bans fake reviews, paying for reviews conditioned on sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews and certain review suppression. Family members may review a business only with clear disclosure. Hosting honest reviews is generally fine, with no duty to screen each one.

## Children

COPPA protects children under 13. It covers sites directed to children, and general-audience sites only when they actually know a user is under 13.

- A general-audience site doesn't have to ask anyone's age.
- If it does ask, the screen should be neutral. The FTC's FAQ says a checkbox such as "I am over 12" isn't neutral.
- So on a US-facing general-audience site, leave age out of a sign-up box, or use a neutral date-of-birth question.
- Anything aimed at children needs a lawyer. The rule was amended on April 22, 2025, and those changes weren't reviewed here.

## Privacy

There's no single federal privacy law for small sites. A privacy page should still be accurate, because a false promise can be a deceptive practice (from memory, widely accepted).

- **California's CalOPPA:** commercial sites collecting Californians' personal details generally need a posted privacy policy. Its exact scope wasn't read.
- **California's CCPA:** applies to for-profit businesses meeting one test: $26.625 million or more in revenue, data on 100,000 or more California residents or households, or half of revenue from selling or sharing data. Most small sites are under these, but check.
- **Other states:** many have their own privacy laws and thresholds, which weren't read.

## Sales tax

These points come from tax firms and vendors, not state pages, so the client's accountant confirms them.

- Most states with a sales tax tax remote sellers past an "economic nexus" threshold, often $100,000 in sales or 200 transactions. Thresholds and what counts vary by state.
- The seller's home state usually applies regardless.
- The checkout's tax setting wasn't tested. See `07-commerce.md`.
